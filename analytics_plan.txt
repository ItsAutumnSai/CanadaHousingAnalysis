CANADA HOUSING MARKET - ANALYTICS PLAN (DRAFT)
==============================================

Purpose
-------
Questions the dashboard can answer, grouped into the three levels of
business analytics:

  1. Descriptive  - What does the market look like?        (mostly done in the dashboard)
  2. Diagnostic   - Why do prices differ?                  (next step)
  3. Predictive   - What price should we expect?           (later step)

Data limits to keep in mind for every question
----------------------------------------------
- Snapshot only: no listing/sale dates, so no trends over time and no
  forecasting of future prices. "Predictive" here means estimating the
  price of a home from its characteristics, not predicting the market.
- Asking prices, not sale prices.
- British Columbia has more than half the listings. Manitoba (68), Yukon (40)
  and the Northwest Territories (13) are too small to trust on their own.
  Nunavut has no data.
- Lot size (Acreage) is missing for about half the listings (stored as 0).
- Heating, Basement, Exterior, Flooring and Roof are often missing
  (Roof is reported for only about 20% of listings).
- Prices are skewed by a few very expensive listings, so use the MEDIAN, not
  the average, when comparing groups.


=====================================================================
1. DESCRIPTIVE ANALYSIS - "What does the market look like?"
=====================================================================
Already covered by the dashboard visuals.

Market size and supply
  D1. How many listings are there in total, and per province?
      -> KPI "Total listings", chart "Listings by province"
  D2. Which cities have the most listings?
      -> Table "Top 15 cities by number of listings"
  D3. Where are listings concentrated geographically?
      -> Geographic Map (heatmap)

Price level
  D4. What is the typical (median) asking price nationally?
      -> KPI "Median price"
  D5. How far apart are the median and the average price, and what does that say
      about expensive outliers?
      -> KPIs "Median price" vs "Average price", "Price distribution" histogram
  D6. Which provinces are the most and least expensive?
      -> Chart "Median price by province"
  D7. Where are the expensive and the affordable areas on the map?
      -> Geographic Map (individual listings colored by price)
  D8. What is the typical price per sqft (or m²), overall and by city?
      -> KPI "Median price / sqft", Top 15 cities table

Housing stock
  D9.  What mix of property types is for sale (houses vs condos, etc.)?
       -> Chart "Property type mix"
  D10. How big are homes by property type (floor area and lot size)?
       -> "Floor area by property type", "Lot size by property type"
  D11. How does the median price change with the number of bedrooms?
       -> "Median price by number of bedrooms"
  D12. How common are features like a garage, fireplace, balcony, pool, waterfront?
       -> "How common is each feature?"
  D13. What are the most common heating, basement, exterior, flooring, roof and
       sewer types?
       -> "Breakdown by home detail"

Suggested descriptive insights to write up
  - "X% of listings are in BC; the median BC price is $Y vs $Z nationally."
  - "Single Family homes make up X% of listings; condos make up Y%."
  - "The average price is X% higher than the median, showing a small number of
     very expensive listings pull the average up."


=====================================================================
2. DIAGNOSTIC ANALYSIS - "Why do prices differ?"
=====================================================================
Goal: explain the differences seen in the descriptive part. Main tools are
comparisons of medians between groups, correlation, and cross-tabs. Remember:
these show RELATIONSHIPS, not proof of cause.

Location
  G1. Are BC/Ontario homes more expensive because they are bigger, or because the
      location costs more?
      -> Compare median price per sqft by province (removes the size effect).
  G2. Within one province, how much do prices differ between cities?
      (e.g. Vancouver vs Kelowna vs Prince George)
      -> Median price and price per sqft by city, filtered to one province.
  G3. Do urban areas (big cities) cost more per sqft than small towns?
      -> Group cities by number of listings (large / medium / small) and compare.

Size and layout
  G4. How strongly is price related to floor area, bedrooms and bathrooms?
      -> Correlation table (Price vs Square Footage, Bedrooms, Bathrooms, Acreage).
  G5. Does each extra bedroom add a steady amount, or does the gain level off?
      -> Price per bedroom step (1->2, 2->3, ...).
  G6. Is a big lot (acreage) linked to higher prices, or are big lots mostly in
      cheap rural areas?
      -> Price vs lot size, split by province or urban/rural.

Property type
  G7. Why are condos cheaper than single-family homes - is it only size?
      -> Compare price per sqft by property type.
  G8. Does the property type mix explain why some provinces look cheaper?
      (e.g. a province with many condos will have a lower median)
      -> Median price by province AND property type (cross-tab / heatmap table).

Features
  G9.  How much higher is the median price of homes WITH vs WITHOUT a garage,
       fireplace, balcony, pool, waterfront?
       -> "Feature premium" comparison (removed from dashboard for now; add back here).
  G10. Is the feature premium real, or is it because those homes are bigger or in
       pricier areas?
       -> Repeat G9 within one property type and one province, or compare price per sqft.
  G11. Are waterfront homes concentrated in specific regions that are already expensive?
       -> Map of waterfront listings + their province breakdown.
  G12. Do heating type or basement type relate to price? (e.g. heat pump homes being
       newer/more expensive)
       -> Median price by Heating / Basement category.

Suggested diagnostic visuals to add later
  - Correlation heatmap of the numeric columns
  - Median price per sqft by province
  - Province x Property Type table of median prices
  - Feature premium bar chart (with vs without, %)
  - Box plots of price by category (Heating, Basement, Exterior)


=====================================================================
3. PREDICTIVE ANALYSIS - "What price should we expect?"
=====================================================================
Goal: build a model that estimates a listing's asking price from its
characteristics. NOTE: no dates means we cannot forecast future prices.

Business questions
  P1. Given a home's province, city, type, size, bedrooms, bathrooms and
      features, what asking price would we expect?
      -> Price estimator form in the dashboard.
  P2. Which listings look overpriced or underpriced compared with similar homes?
      -> Compare actual price vs predicted price; flag big gaps.
      (Useful for buyers/investors looking for "deals".)
  P3. Which factors matter most for price?
      -> Feature importance from the model (e.g. location vs size vs features).
  P4. How much value would adding a feature (e.g. a garage or extra bathroom)
      add, according to the model?
      -> "What-if" using the estimator: change one input, keep the rest the same.
  P5. Can we classify a listing into a price band (budget / mid / luxury)?
      -> Simple classification as an alternative to predicting the exact price.

Possible approach (keep simple and explainable)
  - Inputs: Province, Property Type, Bedrooms, Bathrooms, Square Footage, Acreage,
    Garage, Parking, Fireplace, Waterfront, Pool, Garden, Balcony,
    (optionally Latitude/Longitude for location)
  - Target: Price (use log of price because prices are very skewed)
  - Models to try:
      1) Linear Regression   - baseline, easy to explain (each input has a +/- effect)
      2) Random Forest       - usually more accurate, gives feature importance
  - Split data 80% train / 20% test
  - Measure: R-squared (how much of the price variation the model explains) and
    MAE (average error in dollars), compared against a simple baseline
    such as "predict the median price of that province"
  - Remove extreme outliers first (e.g. listings above the 99th percentile)

Risks / things to mention in the presentation
  - Model predicts ASKING price, not the final sale price.
  - Weak for provinces with very few listings (MB, YT, NT).
  - Missing details (roof, flooring, ...) are left out of the model, so its
    accuracy is limited.
  - It shows what similar homes are listed for; it is not an official valuation.
